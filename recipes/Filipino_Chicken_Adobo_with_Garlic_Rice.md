# Filipino Chicken Adobo with Garlic Rice

*Filipino · weeknight · 40 min (10 min active) · ~47g protein · ~580 cal per serving*

> **Brand-new cuisine — the kitchen has zero Filipino data.** This is the national dish of the Philippines and it is almost entirely pantry: soy sauce, vinegar, garlic, bay, peppercorns. Nothing to source, nothing to substitute, and it is one of the very few dishes that is *unambiguously better* on day three than day one.

## Ingredients (2 servings)

**Adobo**
- 1½ lbs bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs *(boneless works — see the note)*
- 1/2 cup soy sauce
- 1/3 cup **cane vinegar** — *if unavailable: white vinegar or apple cider vinegar. Do not use balsamic or red wine vinegar.*
- 1/2 cup water
- **8 cloves garlic**, smashed — yes, eight; this is not a typo
- 3 bay leaves
- 1 tsp whole black peppercorns
- 1 tbsp brown sugar
- 1 tbsp neutral oil

**Garlic rice**
- 1 cup white rice (dry), cooked and ideally cooled
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt

**To serve**
- 3 green onions, sliced
- 1 lime, cut into wedges *(optional but good)*

## Method

1. **Marinate — 30 min, or skip it.** Combine the chicken, soy sauce, vinegar, water, smashed garlic, bay leaves, peppercorns and brown sugar in a bowl or zip bag. Thirty minutes on the counter is plenty. If you're short on time, go straight to step 2 — the braise does most of the work anyway.
2. **Sear.** Lift the chicken out (reserve the marinade — you need all of it). Pat the pieces dry. Heat the oil in a wide skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high and sear the thighs **skin-side down for 5 minutes** until deeply browned. Flip for 2 more.
3. **Braise.** Pour in the entire reserved marinade including the garlic, bay and peppercorns. Bring to a boil, then drop to a low simmer, **cover, and cook 20 minutes.**
4. **Reduce — this is the step that makes it.** Uncover, flip the chicken skin-side up, and simmer hard for another **10–12 minutes** until the liquid has reduced by about two-thirds into a glossy, dark, salty-sour glaze that coats the back of a spoon. Spoon it over the chicken a few times as it thickens. Chicken should read **175°F** — dark meat wants to go past 165°F.
5. **Garlic rice.** While it reduces, heat 2 tbsp oil in a separate pan over medium. Add the minced garlic and cook **60–90 seconds until pale gold — not brown**, or it turns bitter. Add the cooked rice and the salt, and toss until every grain is coated and heated through.
6. **Serve** chicken and sauce over the garlic rice, green onions on top, lime on the side.

## Notes

- **Reheat: microwave, covered, 2–3 min**, or in a skillet over medium with a splash of water. **This is the best reheat on this week's board.** Adobo is a preserved dish by design — the vinegar and soy are why it exists — and it genuinely improves for three days. Make no effort to eat it all on night one.
- **Vinegar rule: do not stir the pot for the first two minutes after the vinegar goes in.** Letting it boil off its raw edge undisturbed is the traditional technique and it does make a difference.
- **Boneless thighs** work fine: cut the covered simmer to 12 minutes and the reduction to 8. Bone-in and skin-on gives a richer sauce.
- **Chicken is a perishable raw protein.** If this lands Thursday or later, freeze the package the day you shop and move it to the fridge the night before.
- Day-old cold rice fries much better than fresh hot rice. If you can, cook the rice the night before.
