# Cajun Honey-Butter Shrimp Bowls
*Cajun/Creole · **Weekend — best eaten fresh** · 25 min · ~40g protein · ~525 cal per serving*

## Ingredients (2 servings)
- 1 lb large shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 1 cup jasmine rice (dry)
- 2 tbsp cornstarch
- 2 tbsp butter
- 2 tbsp **honey**
- 1 tbsp **Cajun seasoning**
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 green onions, sliced
- 1 bell pepper, diced
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp chopped parsley (optional garnish)

## Method
1. Cook jasmine rice: 1 cup rice + 1 1/2 cups water, covered, 15 min; rest 5 min.
2. Pat shrimp **very** dry; toss with Cajun seasoning, 1/2 tsp salt and 1/4 tsp pepper, then dust with the **cornstarch** and toss again until lightly and evenly coated.
3. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high. Add bell pepper **seasoned first with a pinch of salt, pepper and a shake of the Cajun blend** — season the peppers *before* the pre-cook, not after; unseasoned peppers go in bland and dull everything around them. Cook 3 min until softened at the edges but still with bite. Remove to a plate.
4. Add shrimp to the empty skillet in a single layer — work in two batches if they crowd. Sear 1 1/2–2 min per side until pink, opaque and lightly crisp at the edges. Add garlic in the last 30 seconds.
5. Lower heat to medium. Return the peppers. Add butter and honey; swirl until melted into a glossy sauce coating the shrimp. Finish with lemon juice.
6. Serve over rice, topped with green onions and parsley.

## Notes
- **Reheat: don't, if you can help it — eat this one the night you make it.** Shrimp goes rubbery and tough in a microwave, which is the whole reason this dish sits on a weekend slot instead of a weeknight. If you must reheat, use a skillet over medium-low with a teaspoon of water, 60–90 seconds, and pull it the moment it's warm. Cook the rice fresh alongside rather than reheating the bowl assembled.
- **The cornstarch dredge is the upgrade** — the same trick that got called out by name on the Bang Bang Salmon. It gives the shrimp a thin crisp shell that the honey-butter clings to instead of sliding off. Dry shrimp and a hot pan are both required for it to work.
- Don't overcook the shrimp — they're done the moment they curl into a loose "C." A tight "O" means you went too far.
- Cajun blends vary wildly in salt; taste before adding the full teaspoon.
- The honey-butter glaze plays sweet against the Cajun heat — adjust honey to taste.
