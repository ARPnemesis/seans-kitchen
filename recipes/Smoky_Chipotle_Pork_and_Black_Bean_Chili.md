# Smoky Chipotle Pork & Black Bean Chili

*Tex-Mex / American comfort · weeknight · 40 min · ~46g protein · ~545 cal per serving*

> American comfort is the broadest winner on your board (5★ average across six dishes) and there has never been a chili on the menu in nine weeks. Ground pork keeps it off the beef rotation, and chili is the single best reheating format in existence — it is genuinely better on day two.

## Ingredients (2 generous servings, plus leftovers)

**Chili**
- 1 lb ground pork *(if unavailable: 1 lb 85/15 ground beef — the dish works either way)*
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 yellow onion, diced
- 1 red bell pepper, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 chipotle peppers in adobo, minced, **+ 1 tbsp of the adobo sauce** *(this is the smoke and the heat — see the note)*
- 1 tbsp chili powder
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 1 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 can (14.5 oz) fire-roasted diced tomatoes
- 1 1/2 cups chicken stock
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp masa harina *(or 1 tbsp cornmeal — thickens and adds a corn note; optional)*

**To finish**
- 1/2 cup shredded cheddar
- 1/4 cup sour cream
- 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
- 1 lime, cut into wedges
- 3 green onions, sliced

## Method

1. **Brown the pork hard.** Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or heavy pot over medium-high. Add the ground pork and break it into large pieces. Cook 6–7 minutes **without stirring much** — you want real browned crust on the bottom of the chunks, not grey crumbles. Season with half the salt. Move it to a bowl, leaving the fat.
2. **Vegetables.** Drop to medium. Add the onion and bell pepper to the pork fat and cook 5 minutes until softened. Garlic in for 30 seconds.
3. **Bloom the spices.** Add the chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika, oregano, tomato paste and the minced chipotles with their adobo. Stir constantly for **60 seconds** — the spices should smell toasty and the paste should darken. This step is the difference between a chili that tastes deep and one that tastes dusty.
4. **Build.** Return the pork and its juices. Add the black beans, fire-roasted tomatoes and chicken stock. Scrape the bottom of the pot completely clean. Bring to a boil, then drop to a bare simmer.
5. **Simmer 20–25 minutes, uncovered**, stirring occasionally, until it has thickened to a spoonable stew rather than a soup. If you're using masa harina, whisk it with 2 tbsp of water and stir it in for the last 5 minutes.
6. **Taste and adjust.** Remaining salt, black pepper, and a squeeze of lime. Chili almost always wants more salt and more acid than you expect.
7. **Serve** in bowls with cheddar, sour cream, cilantro, green onion and a lime wedge.

## Notes

- **Reheat: microwave, covered, 2–3 min**, or on the stovetop over medium with a splash of stock. This is the best reheat on this week's menu — chili is *better* on day two once the spices have married. Toppings go on fresh each time; never store the sour cream stirred in.
- **Chipotles in adobo come in a 7 oz can and you're using two peppers.** Freeze the rest in a small bag, flattened — they keep for months and you'll use them again.
- **Heat control:** two chipotles + the adobo lands at a solid medium heat. One pepper is mild; three is hot. Scrape the seeds out of the peppers to keep the smoke and lose some burn.
- Ground pork is the whole point of using pork here — it browns richer than turkey and lighter than beef. If King Soopers is out, 85/15 ground beef is the sanctioned swap, no other changes needed.
- Freezes beautifully for up to 3 months. Cool completely before it goes in.
