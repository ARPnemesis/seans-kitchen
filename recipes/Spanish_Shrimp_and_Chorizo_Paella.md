# Spanish Shrimp & Chorizo Paella

*Spanish · weekend · 45 min · ~43g protein · ~590 cal*

## Ingredients (2 servings)

- 3/4 lb large shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 6 oz Spanish chorizo (cured), sliced into coins
- 1 cup bomba or arborio rice
- 2½ cups chicken stock
- 1 pinch saffron threads (~¼ tsp), crumbled
- 1 yellow onion, diced
- 1 red bell pepper, sliced into strips
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 (14.5 oz) can diced tomatoes, drained
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- ½ cup frozen peas
- 1 lemon, cut in wedges
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped

## Method

1. Warm the chicken stock in a small saucepan and crumble the saffron into it. Let it steep off the heat — this is what colors and perfumes the rice, so give it 10 minutes.
2. Heat the olive oil in a wide skillet or paella pan over medium. Add the chorizo and cook 3–4 minutes until it renders its orange fat and the edges crisp. Remove to a plate, leaving the fat.
3. Add the onion and red pepper to the fat and cook 6 minutes until soft. Add the garlic and smoked paprika for 30 seconds, then the drained tomatoes. Cook 3–4 minutes until the mixture darkens and thickens into a rough sofrito.
4. Stir in the rice and toast it for 2 minutes, coating every grain in the sofrito.
5. Pour in the saffron stock, add the salt and pepper, and stir once to level the rice. **Do not stir again** — the undisturbed bottom layer is what forms the socarrat, the toasted crust that makes this dish.
6. Simmer uncovered on medium-low 15 minutes. Scatter the shrimp and peas over the top, tuck the chorizo back in, and cook 6–8 more minutes until the shrimp are pink and the liquid is gone.
7. Raise the heat to medium-high for 60–90 seconds. Listen for a crackle from the bottom — that's the socarrat forming. Pull it before it smells burnt.
8. Rest off the heat 5 minutes, covered with a towel. Finish with parsley and lemon wedges.

## Notes
- Spanish cured chorizo, not Mexican raw chorizo — they behave completely differently. Cured is in the deli or specialty section.
- Bomba rice absorbs three times its volume without turning to mush. Arborio is the best backup; long-grain will not work.
- Resist stirring. Every stir you skip is more socarrat.
- A weekend dish by design — it wants an unhurried 45 minutes and a wide pan.
