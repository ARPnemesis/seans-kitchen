# Vietnamese Lemongrass Pork Meatball Bowls

*Vietnamese · weeknight · 30 min · ~40g protein · ~540 cal*

## Ingredients (2 servings)

**Meatballs**
- 1 lb ground pork
- 2 stalks lemongrass, tough outer layers removed, tender core minced fine (or 2 tbsp lemongrass paste)
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 shallot, minced
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tbsp honey
- 1 tsp cornstarch
- ½ tsp black pepper
- ¼ tsp salt
- 1 tbsp neutral oil, for the pan

**Nuoc cham (dipping sauce)**
- 3 tbsp fish sauce
- 3 tbsp lime juice
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 4 tbsp warm water
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 red chili, thinly sliced (optional)

**Bowls**
- 6 oz rice vermicelli noodles
- 1 English cucumber, julienned or sliced thin
- 2 carrots, julienned
- 2 cups butter lettuce or green leaf lettuce, torn
- ¼ cup fresh mint leaves
- ¼ cup fresh cilantro
- 2 green onions, sliced

## Method

1. Whisk the nuoc cham ingredients together until the sugar dissolves. Taste — it should read sweet, sour, salty, and sharp in roughly equal measure. Set aside.
2. Combine the pork, lemongrass, garlic, shallot, fish sauce, honey, cornstarch, pepper, and salt. Mix with your hands just until it comes together — overworking makes them dense. Form into ~16 small meatballs and flatten each slightly into a puck (more surface area, more caramelization).
3. Cook the vermicelli per the package — usually a 5-minute soak in boiling water. Drain, rinse under cold water, and set aside.
4. Heat the neutral oil in a large skillet over medium-high. Cook the meatballs in a single layer 3 minutes per side, until deeply caramelized and cooked through. Work in two batches rather than crowding them.
5. Build the bowls: noodles on the bottom, then lettuce, cucumber, carrot, meatballs. Pile on the mint, cilantro, and green onion.
6. Pour nuoc cham over the bowl at the table — a few tablespoons each, more as you go.

## Notes
- The nuoc cham is not a garnish. It's the dressing, and the bowl is under-seasoned without a generous pour.
- Fresh lemongrass is worth the trip; the paste in a tube is a fair substitute, jarred minced is a distant third.
- Ground pork gives the best texture, but ground chicken or turkey works with an extra teaspoon of oil in the mix.
- Herbs are structural here, not decorative — use the full amount.
