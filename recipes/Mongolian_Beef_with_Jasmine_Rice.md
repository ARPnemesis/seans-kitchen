# Mongolian Beef with Jasmine Rice & Charred Scallions

*Chinese · weekend (or any weeknight) · 30 min · ~45g protein · ~590 cal per serving*

> 🧊 **FREEZE THE STEAK ON ARRIVAL — thaw in the fridge the night before.** This one is genuinely easier that way: **flank steak slices far cleaner when it's semi-frozen**, so the freeze isn't a compromise, it's the technique. It also keeps raw beef out of a five-day fridge stay, which is what cost you a dinner on 08-09.
>
> **Brand-new cuisine — the kitchen has zero Chinese data.** Your two closest neighbors both landed: Korean bulgogi 5★, gochujang turkey bowl 4★. This is the same saucy-beef-over-rice shape that works for you, and unlike a dry stir-fry it reheats properly because the sauce protects the meat.

## Ingredients (2 servings)

**Beef**
- 1 lb **flank steak**, sliced **¼-inch thick against the grain** — *if unavailable: skirt steak or sirloin, same treatment*
- 3 tbsp cornstarch
- 1/4 tsp kosher salt
- 3 tbsp neutral oil, for searing

**Sauce**
- 1/2 cup low-sodium soy sauce
- 1/3 cup **brown sugar**, packed
- 1/2 cup water
- 1 tbsp **rice vinegar** *(check you have enough — you have run short on this before)*
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 5 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
- 1/2 tsp chili flakes *(optional)*
- 1 tbsp cornstarch + 2 tbsp cold water, whisked into a slurry

**To serve**
- 1 cup jasmine rice (dry)
- **8 oz broccoli florets** — *this is the ceiling. The protein and sauce lead here.*
- 6 green onions, cut into 2-inch batons
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds

## Method

1. **Rice on.** 1 cup jasmine rice + 1½ cups water, covered, 15 min; rest 5 min off heat.
2. **Slice the beef properly.** If it's still part-frozen, this is easy — cut **against the grain** into ¼-inch strips. Against the grain is not optional on flank; with the grain it eats like rope.
3. **Cornstarch coat.** Toss the beef with the 3 tbsp cornstarch and the salt until every strip is dusted and dry to the touch. *(This is your own crust trick from the Bang Bang salmon, doing double duty — it crisps the surface and it thickens the sauce as it cooks.)*
4. **Whisk the sauce:** soy, brown sugar, water, rice vinegar, ginger, garlic, sesame oil and chili flakes. Set the cornstarch slurry aside separately.
5. **Sear in two batches — this is the one place you can ruin it.** Heat 1½ tbsp oil in a large skillet or wok over high until it shimmers. Add half the beef in a single layer and **do not touch it for 90 seconds.** Toss for another 30–60 seconds until browned, then remove. Repeat with the rest. Crowding the pan steams the beef grey.
6. **Broccoli.** Add the broccoli to the hot pan with 2 tbsp water, cover, and steam 3 minutes until bright green and just tender. Remove.
7. **Sauce and finish.** Pour the sauce into the pan and boil 2 minutes, scraping the bottom. Whisk in the slurry and cook 60 seconds until it turns glossy and coats a spoon. Return the beef and broccoli, add the green onion batons, and toss for 1 minute — just enough to char the scallions at the edges and coat everything.
8. **Serve** over jasmine rice with sesame seeds.

## Notes

- **Reheat: microwave, covered, 2 min**, or a skillet over medium-low with a splash of water. **Because the beef is coated in sauce rather than dry-seared naked, it holds up far better than a typical stir-fry** — this is a real next-day lunch, not a compromise.
- **Do not skip the two-batch sear.** One crowded batch is the difference between Mongolian beef and grey beef soup.
- **Sweetness dial:** 1/3 cup brown sugar is the restaurant standard and it is sweet. Take it to 1/4 cup if you'd rather it lean savory.
- **Fallback protein:** skirt or sirloin both work. Avoid stew meat or chuck — this is a fast-sear cut, not a braising cut.
- If you like it hotter, the chili flakes go in the sauce rather than on top so they bloom.
