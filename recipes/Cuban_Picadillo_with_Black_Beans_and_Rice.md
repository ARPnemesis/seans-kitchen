# Cuban Picadillo with Black Beans & Rice
*Cuban · Weeknight · 35 min · ~42g protein · ~575 cal per serving*

## Ingredients (2 servings)

**Picadillo**
- 1 lb lean ground beef (93/7)
- 1 yellow onion, diced
- 1 green bell pepper, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 can (8 oz) tomato sauce
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1/3 cup pimiento-stuffed green olives, sliced
- 2 tbsp capers, drained
- 1/3 cup raisins
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 bay leaf
- 1/4 cup chicken stock
- 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper

**To serve**
- 1 cup white rice (dry)
- 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 lime, cut into wedges
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh cilantro

## Method
1. Start the rice: 1 cup rice + 1 1/2 cups water, covered, 15 min; rest 5 min off heat, then fluff.
2. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium. Add onion and bell pepper with 1/2 tsp of the salt and cook 6–7 min until soft and sweet — this is the sofrito, and rushing it is the one way to make this dish taste flat. Add garlic; cook 1 min.
3. Raise heat to medium-high. Add ground beef, breaking it up. Cook 6–8 min until browned and no pink remains. Drain excess fat if there's more than a tablespoon.
4. Stir in tomato paste, cumin, oregano, smoked paprika, remaining 1/2 tsp salt and the pepper. Cook 1 min until the paste darkens.
5. Add tomato sauce, chicken stock, raisins and the bay leaf. Simmer uncovered 12–15 min, stirring occasionally, until thickened and glossy — it should mound on a spoon, not run.
6. Off heat, stir in olives, capers and the red wine vinegar. Taste and adjust salt. Discard the bay leaf.
7. Warm the black beans in a small pot with a pinch of salt and a splash of their liquid.
8. Serve picadillo over rice with beans alongside. Finish with cilantro and a hard squeeze of lime.

## Notes
- **Reheat: microwave, covered, 2 min**, stirring once halfway. Picadillo is one of the rare dishes that is genuinely better on day two — the raisins and olives keep sweetening into the sauce overnight. Keep the rice in a separate container and add a teaspoon of water before reheating it.
- The sweet–salty–briny triangle (raisins, olives, capers) *is* the dish. Leaving out the raisins because they sound odd in beef is the most common way people end up with plain taco meat.
- The vinegar goes in off the heat. Simmered, it cooks off and you lose the brightness that cuts the richness.
- Excellent second life: fold leftovers into a baked sweet potato, or into an omelet with a little cheese.
