# Hungarian Beef Goulash with Buttered Egg Noodles

*Hungarian · weekend · 2 hr 15 min (25 min active) · ~48g protein · ~635 cal*

> 🧊 **FREEZE ON ARRIVAL — move the beef to the fridge the night before you cook.**
> This is the second attempt at this dish. The first never happened: the stew meat sat in the fridge for five days and was rancid by the cook date. The recipe was never at fault and you said "cook again: yes." **Buy a chuck roast, put it straight in the freezer the day you shop, and thaw it in the fridge the night before.** That single step is the whole fix.

**Reheat rating: best on the menu.** Goulash is famously better the second day, and this makes generous portions — expect leftovers beyond the two servings.

## Ingredients (2 generous servings, with extra stew)

**Goulash**
- 1½ lb **beef chuck roast**, cut into 1½-inch cubes — **buy a whole roast and cube it yourself; do not buy pre-cut "stew meat."** The pre-cut trays are trimmings of unknown age from unknown cuts, which is exactly what failed last time.
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil
- 2 yellow onions, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 red bell pepper, cut into 1-inch pieces
- **3 tbsp sweet Hungarian paprika** — this is the entire personality of the dish, not a pinch. *If unavailable: regular sweet paprika works; smoked paprika does NOT — it takes the dish somewhere else entirely.*
- 1 tsp caraway seeds, lightly crushed
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 3 cups beef broth
- 2 medium carrots, cut into thick coins
- 1 bay leaf

**To serve**
- 8 oz wide egg noodles
- 3 tbsp butter
- ¼ cup sour cream
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
- 1 crusty baguette, warmed and sliced

## Method

1. **Thaw properly.** If the beef went into the freezer on shopping day (it should have), move it to the fridge the night before. Never thaw it on the counter.
2. **Dry and dredge.** Pat the beef cubes very dry — wet meat steams instead of browning. Season with the salt and pepper, then toss with the flour until lightly coated.
3. **Sear hard.** Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or heavy pot over medium-high. Brown the beef in two batches, 3 minutes per side, until each piece has a real crust. Crowding the pot is the one mistake that costs you the whole dish. Move the beef to a bowl.
4. **Onions.** Drop to medium. Add the onions to the pot and cook 8 minutes, scraping up the browned bits as they release. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds.
5. **Paprika off the heat.** *Pull the pot off the burner*, then add the paprika, caraway, and tomato paste and stir for 30 seconds. Paprika scorches and turns bitter over direct high heat — this off-heat step is the traditional fix and it matters.
6. **Deglaze and braise.** Return to the heat, pour in the beef broth, and scrape the bottom completely clean. Return the beef and its juices, add the bay leaf, bring to a bare simmer, cover, and cook on low for **1 hour 30 minutes**.
7. **Add the vegetables.** Stir in the carrots and bell pepper. Cover and continue 30–40 minutes more. **Doneness cue: the beef should shred under light pressure from a spoon, with no springiness.** If it still resists, it needs more time, not more heat — chuck goes tender on a schedule of its own.
8. **Reduce if needed.** Uncover and simmer 10 minutes to thicken to a gravy that coats. Fish out the bay leaf. Taste for salt.
9. **Noodles.** Boil the egg noodles in well-salted water to just-tender, drain, and toss with the butter and half the parsley.
10. **Serve.** Noodles in the bowl, goulash over them, a spoonful of sour cream, remaining parsley. Stand warmed baguette slices around the rim to soak up the sauce.

## Notes

- **Reheat: stovetop, medium-low, 6–8 min with a splash of broth** — or microwave, covered, 3 min. Add the sour cream fresh each time; never reheat it stirred in. **Day two and three is where goulash lives.** Freezes well for up to 3 months.
- **Buy sweet Hungarian paprika specifically** if you can find it — regular supermarket paprika is duller and you'll taste the difference at 3 tablespoons.
- **Vegetable ratio:** one bell pepper and two carrots across a pot this size is deliberate. The beef and the paprika gravy lead; the vegetables are texture.
- The sour cream goes on at the bowl, never stirred into the simmering pot — it will split.
- If you'd rather not freeze: buy the chuck roast midweek instead, on a separate trip. Either solution works; leaving raw beef in the fridge from Saturday to the following weekend does not.
