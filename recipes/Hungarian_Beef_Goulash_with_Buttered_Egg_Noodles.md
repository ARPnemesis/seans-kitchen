# Hungarian Beef Goulash with Buttered Egg Noodles

*Hungarian · weekend · 2 hr 15 min (25 min active) · ~46g protein · ~595 cal*

**Reheat rating: best on the menu.** Goulash is famously better the second day, and this makes generous portions — expect leftovers beyond the two servings.

## Ingredients (2 generous servings, with extra stew)

**Goulash**
- 1½ lb beef chuck roast, cut into 1½-inch cubes
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil
- 2 yellow onions, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 red bell peppers, cut into 1-inch pieces
- **3 tbsp sweet Hungarian paprika** — this is the entire personality of the dish, not a pinch
- 1 tsp caraway seeds, lightly crushed
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 3 cups beef broth
- 2 medium carrots, cut into thick coins
- 1 bay leaf

**To serve**
- 8 oz wide egg noodles
- 3 tbsp butter
- ¼ cup sour cream
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
- 1 crusty baguette, warmed and sliced

## Method

1. **Dry and dredge.** Pat the beef cubes very dry — wet meat steams instead of browning. Season with the salt and pepper, then toss with the flour until lightly coated.
2. **Sear hard.** Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or heavy pot over medium-high. Brown the beef in two batches, 3 minutes per side, until each piece has a real crust. Crowding the pot is the one mistake that costs you the whole dish. Move the beef to a bowl.
3. **Onions.** Drop to medium. Add the onions to the pot and cook 8 minutes, scraping up the browned bits as they release. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds.
4. **Paprika off the heat.** *Pull the pot off the burner*, then add the paprika, caraway, and tomato paste and stir for 30 seconds. Paprika scorches and turns bitter over direct high heat — this off-heat step is the traditional fix and it matters.
5. **Deglaze and braise.** Return to the heat, pour in the beef broth, and scrape the bottom completely clean. Return the beef and its juices, add the bay leaf, bring to a bare simmer, cover, and cook on low for **1 hour 30 minutes**.
6. **Add the vegetables.** Stir in the carrots and bell peppers. Cover and continue 30–40 minutes more. **Doneness cue: the beef should shred under light pressure from a spoon, with no springiness.** If it still resists, it needs more time, not more heat — chuck goes tender on a schedule of its own.
7. **Reduce if needed.** Uncover and simmer 10 minutes to thicken to a gravy that coats. Fish out the bay leaf. Taste for salt.
8. **Noodles.** Boil the egg noodles in well-salted water to just-tender, drain, and toss with the butter and half the parsley.
9. **Serve.** Noodles in the bowl, goulash over them, a spoonful of sour cream, remaining parsley. Stand warmed baguette slices around the rim to soak up the sauce.

## Notes

- **Buy sweet Hungarian paprika specifically** if you can find it — regular supermarket paprika is duller and you'll taste the difference at 3 tablespoons. Smoked paprika is *not* a substitute here; it takes the dish somewhere else entirely.
- Chuck is the right cut. Stew meat of unknown origin is a gamble; a chuck roast you cube yourself is cheaper and more consistent.
- The sour cream goes on at the bowl, never stirred into the simmering pot — it will split.
- **Day two and three:** this is where goulash lives. Reheat gently on the stove; add the sour cream fresh each time. Freezes well for up to 3 months.
