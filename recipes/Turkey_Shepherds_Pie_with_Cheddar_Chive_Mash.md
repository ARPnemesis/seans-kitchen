# Turkey Shepherd's Pie with Cheddar-Chive Mash

*British comfort · weekend · 55 min · ~45g protein · ~580 cal · **yield: 3 meals** (1 dinner + 2 lunches)*

> 🥔 **Two things this dish is doing on purpose.** First, oven-baked casseroles are the best-reheating format this kitchen has on record — the Baked Rigatoni and the Cottage Cheese Ziti are 2-for-2 at 5★ and you called the Rigatoni "one dinner and two lunches." Second, you asked for **mashed potatoes** back on the table. Here they are, as the roof.

## Ingredients (2 generous servings — makes 3 meals)

**Filling**
- 1¼ lb 93/7 ground turkey — 🧊 **FREEZE ON ARRIVAL.** *Ground poultry is the most perishable thing on this week's list and this dish sits at the back of the week. Freeze it the day you shop and move it to the fridge the night before you cook. This is technique, not penance. Sanctioned fallback: 85/15 ground beef, which makes it a cottage pie and is equally good.*
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 yellow onion, diced
- 2 carrots, diced small
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 1½ cups chicken stock
- 1 tbsp **Worcestershire sauce** — *fallback: 2 tsp soy sauce*
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- ½ tsp dried rosemary, crushed
- 1 cup frozen peas
- 1 tsp salt
- ½ tsp black pepper

**Cheddar-chive mash**
- 2 lb russet potatoes, peeled and cut in 2-inch chunks
- 4 tbsp butter
- ⅓ cup whole milk, warmed
- ¾ cup shredded sharp cheddar
- 3 tbsp fresh chives, sliced — *fallback: 2 green onions, green parts only*
- 1 tsp salt
- ½ tsp black pepper

## Method

1. Put the potatoes in a pot, cover with cold salted water, bring to a boil, and cook 15–18 minutes until a knife slides through with no resistance. Drain and let them steam dry in the colander for 2 minutes — wet potatoes make gluey mash.
2. Meanwhile, heat the oil in an oven-safe skillet over medium-high. Brown the turkey 6–7 minutes, breaking it up. Let it sit undisturbed the first two minutes so it crusts.
3. Add the onion and carrot; cook 5 minutes. Add the garlic, thyme, and rosemary; 1 minute.
4. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1 minute until it darkens. Sprinkle over the flour and stir 1 minute more — it should look dry and pasty.
5. Pour in the stock and Worcestershire, scraping the bottom. Simmer 5–7 minutes until it thickens to a gravy that coats a spoon. Stir in the peas, salt, and pepper. Kill the heat.
6. Mash the potatoes with the butter and warm milk until smooth. Fold in the cheddar and chives; season.
7. Spread the mash over the filling in an even layer and drag a fork across the top in ridges — the ridges are what brown.
8. Bake at **400°F for 20–25 minutes**, until the gravy bubbles at the edges and the ridges are golden. Broil 2 minutes if you want more color. **Rest 10 minutes before cutting** or it will slump.

## Notes
- 🔥 **REHEAT: 350°F oven, 15–18 minutes, uncovered.** Uncovered is the point — it re-crisps the potato top, which is the whole reason to make this. Microwave works in a pinch (covered, 2½ minutes) but you lose the roof.
- **Vegetable ceiling:** two carrots and one cup of peas. That's it. Ten ounces of a single supporting vegetable overwhelmed a dish once already this summer — this is deliberately restrained.
- Cut it in thirds, not halves: one dinner plus two lunch portions.
- If you don't have an oven-safe skillet, build it in a 9×9 baking dish.
