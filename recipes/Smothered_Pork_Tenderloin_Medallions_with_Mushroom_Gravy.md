# Smothered Pork Tenderloin Medallions with Mushroom Gravy & Smashed Potatoes

*American comfort · weeknight · 40 min · ~47g protein · ~575 cal*

**Reheat rating: excellent.** Meat sitting in gravy is the single most reliable next-day format there is — the sauce protects the pork from drying out.

**Note on the cut:** this is pork *tenderloin*, sliced into medallions — not a pork chop. Different cut, different texture, no bone, and it finishes in a sauce rather than dry on the plate.

## Ingredients (2 servings)

**Pork & gravy**
- 1 lb pork tenderloin, trimmed of silverskin and cut into 1-inch medallions
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp onion powder
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp butter
- 8 oz cremini mushrooms, sliced
- 1 yellow onion, halved and sliced thin
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 1½ cups beef broth
- 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 3–4 sprigs fresh thyme (or ½ tsp dried)

**Smashed potatoes**
- 1½ lb baby yellow potatoes
- 2 tbsp butter
- ¼ cup whole milk
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped

## Method

1. **Potatoes on first.** Halve any potatoes bigger than a golf ball. Cover with cold salted water, bring to a boil, and simmer 15–18 minutes until a knife slides in with no resistance. Drain, return to the hot pot off heat, add the butter, milk, and salt, and smash with a fork to a rustic — not smooth — texture. Cover to keep warm; stir the parsley in at serving.
2. **Season the pork.** Press the medallions gently to an even ~¾ inch thickness. Toss with the garlic powder, onion powder, smoked paprika, salt, and pepper.
3. **Sear.** Heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high until just smoking. Lay the medallions down in a single layer — work in two batches rather than crowding — and sear 2½–3 minutes per side until deeply browned. **Doneness target: pull them at 140°F on an instant-read thermometer; they carry to 145°F resting, which is medium and still juicy.** Move to a plate and tent with foil.
4. **Mushrooms.** Drop to medium. Add 1 tbsp of the butter, then the mushrooms. Do not stir for the first 3 minutes — let them brown. Then stir and cook another 3 minutes until they've given up their water and taken it back on.
5. **Onions and roux.** Add the remaining butter and the onion, cook 4 minutes until soft. Add the garlic and thyme, 30 seconds. Sprinkle the flour over everything and stir constantly for 1 full minute — you're cooking the raw taste out.
6. **Gravy.** Pour in the beef broth in a slow stream, whisking as you go to keep it smooth. Add the Worcestershire and Dijon. Simmer 4–5 minutes until it coats the back of a spoon. Taste for salt.
7. **Smother.** Return the pork and every drop of its resting juice to the pan, spoon gravy over the top, and simmer 2 minutes to warm through. Serve over the smashed potatoes.

## Notes

- **The thermometer matters here.** Pork tenderloin goes from juicy to chalky in about 90 seconds of overcooking. 140°F off the heat, 145°F after resting.
- Silverskin is the pearly membrane along one side — slide a thin knife under it and pull. It never softens if you leave it on.
- If the gravy tightens too much, loosen it with a splash of broth, not water.
- **Day two:** reheat pork and gravy together, covered, over low heat — the gravy re-loosens and the pork stays tender. Potatoes reheat best with a splash of milk stirred in.
